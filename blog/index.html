Authority before execution — in the open.

Short, technical arguments for one idea: no consequential machine action should execute unless it presents valid, specific, unconsumed authority for that exact action. Below, why that discipline matters now — and why we expect it to become table stakes.

Thesis · 18 Aug 2026

Capability, not identity, is the unit of machine trust

Identity answers who is asking. It never answers whether this exact action, on this exact resource, right now, was authorized. As principals stop being people, that gap stops being academic.

Read → Architecture · 30 Jul 2026

The execution gap: “it had permission” is the wrong question

A standing permission is a broad, ambient claim. Execution is a single, specific act. The interesting engineering lives in the distance between them — and it is only widening.

Read → Design · 9 Jul 2026

Single-use by default: authority that cannot be replayed

A grant that can be presented twice is a grant you have already lost control of. Binding one nonce to one action makes replay a refused event instead of a silent success.

Read → Evidence · 18 Jun 2026

Evidence you can verify without trusting us

Two linked receipts — what was authorized, and what was executed — checkable offline against published keys. The point is to make our word unnecessary.

Read → Outlook · 27 May 2026

Preparing for a world of many agents

The count of non-human principals taking consequential actions only goes one direction. Controls that feel optional at one agent become load-bearing at ten thousand.
