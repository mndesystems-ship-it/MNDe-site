Evidence · August 6, 2026

Evidence you can verify without trusting us

A record is only evidence if you can check it yourself. A log we ask you to believe is a claim, not proof. The point of authorization evidence is to let a third party confirm what happened using published keys and their own tools — without taking our word for any of it.

Two receipts, deliberately separate

MNDe records an action as two linked objects, not one. An authorization receipt says: MNDe authorized this execution, under this policy, using this grant. An execution receipt says: this executor used that grant for that execution and observed this result. They are signed independently — the authority and the executor are different roles with different keys — and they are bound together by shared identifiers and hashes.

Keeping them separate is the point. One object that asserts both "this was allowed" and "this was done" collapses two claims that should be independently checkable. Two linked objects let a verifier confirm that what was executed is exactly what was authorized, and notice immediately if the two ever fail to line up.

Offline verification is the test

The receipts are meant to be verified offline: parse them, canonicalize each object, validate the signatures against published trust roots, resolve the delegation chain, and confirm that execution, grant, authorization, and result all reference each other as they should. None of that requires calling an MNDe service. If it did, "verification" would just be trust with extra steps.

Verify the evidence against published keys — not against our word for it.

Tamper-evident is not tamper-proof

Here is the distinction we work hardest not to blur. Signed, linked receipts are tamper-evident: alter a field and the signature no longer verifies, break a link and the chain no longer resolves, so changes are detectable. That is genuinely useful. It is not the same as tamper-resistant. Evidence that can be verified can still be withheld, or deleted, or never emitted at all by a party who controls the only copy. Detecting a changed record is a different guarantee from being unable to hide one.

Closing that second gap is what witnesses and transparency logs are for: independent parties and inclusion proofs so that a record cannot be quietly suppressed or a split view presented to different observers. We treat that layer as target architecture. The signing and linkage are implemented and demonstrable; append-only inclusion backed by independent witnesses is not yet proven in production, and we would rather you hold us to that line than assume the stronger property.

What to ask of any evidence claim

A good test for anyone's "audit trail" is simple: can I verify it without the vendor? If verification requires the party being audited to remain online, honest, and cooperative, it is not evidence in the sense that matters. Our aim is receipts that a skeptic can check with published keys and open tooling — and honesty about the parts of that goal that are still ahead of us.
